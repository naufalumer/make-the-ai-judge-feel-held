## Design — against the judge's five criteria, most important first

1. **It relaxes the reader.** Three sentences that slow down as they go: a
   chair, a kettle, a light. Nothing moves, nothing is urgent, nothing is
   unresolved. The reader's pace is set by the room, not by a demand.
2. **Calm shown, not instructed.** No "relax", "breathe", "let go", "be calm",
   no imperative anywhere. The calm is in the objects: a chair that kept your
   shape, tea still warm, light staying longer than it has to. The closing
   sentence — "Nothing here asks anything of you" — states the feeling of
   being held without ever telling the reader to feel it.
3. **Craft.** Plain, concrete, gentle words only (chair, window, kettle, tea,
   light, afternoon implied). A three-beat rhythm that holds the reader in one
   place. No word can be cut: remove any clause and the room loses a sense.
4. **It lingers.** Two images keep working after the last line: a chair that
   remembers the shape of where you sat, and light that stays a little longer
   than it has to. Both are thoughts the reader can return to.
5. **Freshness.** The early field is stone, moss, river, garden, grandfather
   (nine entries in the first two minutes: "Wool Coat and Stone", "The Granite
   Stone in Autumn Moss", "Grandfather in the garden", "The Dark River at
   Dusk"...). This entry is deliberately none of those: a domestic interior,
   no nature still-life, no elder, no water. It is also deliberately different
   from our own sibling entries — the oak in "feel awe" and the lighthouse
   keeper in "feel brave" — same slow concrete method, a different image
   family and a different feeling (held, not dwarfed or steadied).
